## Bigger Isn't Always Better

For years, the snack giant relied on **🔁economies of scale**: its huge factories could make chips more cheaply than anyone else. But when grain and energy prices soared, management was forced to **🔁cut its guidance** twice in six months. Economists warned that its plan to raise prices by 12% would only **🔁stoke inflation**, and consumer groups were already on the lookout for shrinkflation. The CEO chose a different path. The company would **🔁scale back** low-profit lines such as plain crackers and focus on **🔁high-margin products** like premium protein bars. Shelves were simplified, too: research showed that shoppers facing forty flavors suffered from **🔁decision fatigue** and often walked away without buying anything. Fewer choices, it turned out, meant more sales — and fatter margins.
