# Morning Story — 2026-10-03 (Day 1)

## The Quiet Cash Pile

When the board of Halden Logistics met on Monday, everyone knew the company was **facing stiff headwinds**: freight rates had slipped for three straight quarters, and a key client in Rotterdam had just delayed its renewal. Investors, however, seemed oddly calm. "The market has already **priced in** a weak fourth quarter," the CFO explained, pointing to a share price that had barely moved after the bad news broke. What worried her more was the next twelve months, and on that front she wanted the company to **tighten the purse strings** now rather than be forced to later — a hiring freeze, fewer consultants, and no new office leases.

The CEO agreed, but only up to a point. Halden had spent five disciplined years building a **war chest** of nearly $400 million, and he had no intention of letting it sit idle while weaker rivals ran out of cash. His compromise was to **hedge his bets**: keep most of the money in reserve to ride out the slowdown, while quietly earmarking a slice of it for the one or two acquisitions that a downturn might put on sale. "If the cycle turns faster than we expect," he told the board, "I'd rather be the buyer than the bargain."

## Today's New Expressions

| # | Expression | 뜻 | Example |
|---|-----------|----|---------|
| 1 | **face (stiff) headwinds** | (사업·경제가) 역풍/불리한 여건에 직면하다 | *The auto industry is facing stiff headwinds from higher interest rates.* |
| 2 | **price in** (sth) | (시장이 어떤 사건·전망을) 가격에 이미 반영하다 | *Investors had largely priced in a rate cut before the announcement.* |
| 3 | **tighten the purse strings** | 지출을 줄이다, 허리띠를 졸라매다 | *With sales falling, the company had to tighten the purse strings.* |
| 4 | **war chest** | (인수·위기 대응 등을 위한) 비축 자금, 실탄 | *The startup raised a $50 million war chest to expand into Asia.* |
| 5 | **hedge one's bets** | (한쪽에 올인하지 않고) 양다리를 걸치다, 위험을 분산하다 | *Many retailers are hedging their bets by sourcing from several countries.* |
